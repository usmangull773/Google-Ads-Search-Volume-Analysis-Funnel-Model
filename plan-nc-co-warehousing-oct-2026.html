What we found

The brief is clear. The risk is in three places where this kind of analysis usually goes wrong, and each one changes the final customer number. Get these right and the model holds up when you take it to an operator or landlord.

A 20 minute drive is not a circle

High impact

A simple radius pulls in areas across highways, rivers or rural stretches that take far longer than 20 minutes to drive, and cuts out areas along fast roads. The boundary will be built from the actual drive time zone, then applied in Keyword Planner as the matching set of ZIP codes, with a radius cross check. You get a screenshot of the exact targeting, plus the ZIP list, so you can confirm it before any numbers are pulled.

Radius only
Includes slow to reach areas, misses areas on fast roads
Drive time ZIP set
Follows the road network, verifiable ZIP by ZIP

"Co-warehousing" alone has thin search demand

High impact

It is a new category, and most small businesses who need it do not search for it by name. The real demand sits in adjacent searches such as small warehouse space, business storage and flex space. Each product gets its own keyword set built from these intent groups, so the volume reflects how buyers actually search.

ProductKeyword groups to pull

Meeting roomsconference room rental, meeting room by the hour, meeting space rental, board room rental

Funnel rates need to be stated, not hidden

Medium impact

The customer estimate is only as good as the rates behind each step. Every rate in the model is shown as an editable assumption with its reasoning, and the action rate changes with price, which is why low and high price points are modelled separately.

Common shortcutThis model
